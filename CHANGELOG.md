# Changelog

## v2.5 — 2026-09-23

Two layers and a projection: what is **around** a property (services), what a municipality or
quarter is **projected to become** (outlook), and how much housing actually **got built** there.

### Population outlook — DST and Københavns Kommune

- **Ten Outlook indicators** per municipality from **DST's `FRKM126`** 2026 vintage, projected to 2040: total growth to 2040 and to 2031, the change per 1 000 inhabitants per year, the absolute change in persons, and the change in the 0–5, 6–16, 20–34 and 80+ cohorts, plus 20–34 measured **against Denmark's own −7.1 %**. Denmark's own 20–34 population falls over the window, so only **7 of 98 municipalities gain any at all** — the absolute map is a wall of red and the relative one is the map that reads, which is why it carries the chip.
- **The same ten again for Copenhagen's 67 quarters and 10 districts**, from **Københavns Kommune's own `KKFR2026`** — a different run, kept in its own file, its own view and its own legend. **They are never spliced**: DST has the city at +5.9 % by 2040 and KK at +8.3 %, and where both appear the gap is stated rather than averaged away.
- **Net dwelling additions** (`hist_net_dwell`) from `BOL101` + `FOLK1A`: what the dwelling stock actually did, per 1 000 inhabitants per year. It sits in **Housing stock**, not Outlook, because it is a measurement. Its window is **six years, not five** — DST publishes no BOL101 for 2021 or 2022 — and the years are read from the table, never hard-coded. It is shown **beside** the projected population rate, each in its own unit, with **nothing computed between them**: persons are not dwellings without a household-size assumption, and this layer makes none.
- **A diverging colour model.** Zero is a real boundary for these indicators — growth and decline are different phenomena — so the ramp runs `hue_neg → paper → hue_pos` with class breaks **mirrored about the centre**, taken from quantiles of the distance from it. That is also the clamp: `fc_abs` runs from −4 617 to +52 670 and a linear ramp would put almost every municipality in one class.
- **A third direction, `neutral`.** A shrinking municipality is not failing and a growing one is not succeeding, so Outlook indicators are never coloured good/bad, never carry the "↓ lower is better" note, and their ranks are positions rather than verdicts. The Analysis sheet's percentile bar reads the same way for them.
- **The year selector says what it has.** An Outlook indicator is one vintage, so the selector is replaced by *"Projection 2026→2040 · DST 2026"* instead of offering years that do not exist.
- **A projected point never looks like an actual.** Area pages carry a Population outlook card with the observed population solid (`FOLK1A` for municipalities, `KKBEF1` for quarters) and the projection dashed to 2040, split by a *today* marker, with "projected" in every projected point's tooltip.
- **The uncertainty reaches the reader.** KK excludes housing plans from the city total by design, but the **split across quarters is driven by the city's unpublished boligprognose**, which KK itself calls *"behæftet med relativ stor usikkerhed"*. That sentence renders wherever a quarter forecast appears. Copenhagen quarter pages also carry one line of past accuracy from a backtest against eight superseded vintages — Nordhavn's +176 % sits next to KK's own **21.8 %** average five-year error for that quarter.
- **Outlook where the decisions are made**: *Outlook around this project* on every infrastructure datasheet, an **Outlook** section on the Analysis sheet for a pinned address, and a **★ Growth story** saved view — Copenhagen by quarter, 20–34 against the city, infrastructure and public buildings on top — that is nothing but a link, so pasting it restores the view exactly.
- 🚫 **What is deliberately not shown.** The **housing gap** needs a fitted household-size trend, which the hard-data rule forbids — it stays research and is in no build target. Copenhagen's **`fc_netmig`** passes the rule but reverses sign against the stock table for five quarters, two of them the city's biggest development sites, so it is not rendered either. `docs/FORECAST.md` §0a lists both, with the reasons.
- **Verified end to end, every data point.** `scripts/verify_forecast_full.py` re-pulls both source tables with its own request, its own parsing and its own arithmetic and diffs them cell by cell: **22 920 cells and 2 008 recomputed indicator values, 0 mismatches** (`docs/DATA_MAP.md` §7e).

### Services overlay — OpenStreetMap and Rejseplanen

- **Services layer**: groceries, food & drink, pharmacies and public-transport stops — **44 181 points across all 99 municipalities**, 2.6 MB of processed JSON. **National from day one**, unlike the public-buildings and schools layers: nothing here is gated behind a per-municipality API pull.
- **Sources chosen by measurement, not assumption** (`docs/SERVICES_PROBE.md`, `scripts/probe_services.py`). The **Geofabrik OSM extract** (495 MB, ODbL 1.0) gives every shop, eating place and pharmacy in **87 s offline**; **Rejseplanen's static GTFS** (55 MB, CC BY 4.0, no key, no registration) gives the stops. **Overpass was rejected as a runtime dependency**: both healthy mirrors returned HTTP 504/429 for minutes at a time, a boundary-by-name lookup timed out every attempt, `overpass.osm.ch` serves no areas database and answered **0 with HTTP 200 for every tag**, and `kumi.systems`' snapshot was 4.5 months stale. Two of those zeros were wrong and were caught only by the extract. **Fødevarestyrelsen's Smiley feed was rejected too** — 58 718 rows with **no coordinate field of any kind**, counting every food *business* (canteens, wholesalers, kiosks) rather than places a resident walks into.
- **Pipeline**: `scripts/fetch_services.py` (stdlib `urllib`, streams to a `.part` file so an interrupted download leaves no truncated pbf, records url/licence/size/fetch-time in a manifest, skips a copy younger than 7 days) → `scripts/build_services.py`. Raw files are gitignored; the processed JSON is committed, so `make build` and CI need neither the 495 MB download nor `osmium`.
- **OSM**: nodes **and** ways, ways placed at their node centroid — **46 % of Danish supermarkets are mapped as building polygons**, so a node-only read loses nearly half the stock. One point per OSM id: 27 objects nationally carry two of our tags (a petrol-station shop that is also a takeaway counter), and the order `grocery → pharmacy → food` decides which category each lands in. `shop=discount` is **0 nationwide** — Netto, Rema 1000, Lidl and Aldi are all `shop=supermarket`.
- **GTFS modes** come from `stops → stop_times → trips → routes`; `stops.txt` says nothing about them. The **extended** route types are mapped deliberately: without `109` and `700` the entire S-train network and 479 bus stops vanish. `715` (flextur) and `4` (ferry) are excluded and counted in the report. A stop serving several modes becomes one point per mode, so Nørreport is a metro point *and* an S-train point *and* a rail point.
- **Station clustering is ours, because the feed has none**: all 36 383 stops carry `location_type=0` and an empty `parent_station`. Same normalised name + same mode within **150 m** (50 m for buses, enough to merge both sides of a street). **Heavy rail barely clusters** — metro, S-train and rail are already one row per station in this feed — while light rail goes 173 → 109 and buses 34 845 → 24 501 (−30 %). Worked examples for Nørreport, København H and Aarhus H in `docs/SERVICES_COUNTS.md`.
- **Sanity checks fail the build**: København groceries **513** (the probe's figure exactly, ±5 % allowed), metro stations **44** (40–50 expected), no municipality without a grocery, and the run stops before committing anything over 15 MB. All four pass at **2.56 MB**.
- **Map overlay** beside *Infra projects* and *Public buildings*, **off by default**, on the same code paths: the same toolbar toggle, the same legend box in the `.maplegs` column, the same *legend is the filter* interaction (click to toggle, shift-click to isolate, *All* to reset), the same two-level popup. Filter chips: **Groceries · Food & drink · Pharmacy · Transport**, with Transport split into **Rail & metro** and **Bus** because those halves are 628 stations against 24 412 stops. Defaults on switch-on: Groceries + Pharmacy + Rail & metro.
- **Performance first.** Zoom floors per category — rail/metro **10**, groceries and pharmacy **13**, food and bus **14** — each with a muted *"Zoom in to see …"* hint naming what is waiting, including the Bus sub-toggle, which would otherwise show nothing and say nothing. Only points **inside the viewport** (padded 15 %) are drawn, and a redraw happens only once the map leaves that padding, so an ordinary pan costs nothing. Per-kommune files are fetched only for bboxes meeting the viewport, capped at 24 per pass and never below the lowest active zoom floor — the national view meets all 99 boxes, and fetching 2.6 MB to draw nothing is what this layer must not do. **Worst case measured: zoom 14 over Nørrebro with every category on is 3 346 markers rebuilt in 15.6 ms, pan 0.4 ms.** No clustering library: the zoom floors already bound it, and `Leaflet.markercluster` would mean a third-party runtime dependency in a page that vendors Leaflet locally.
- **Fix — the dots were rendering under the choropleth.** A canvas renderer's element is created when the renderer is constructed, and `LF.canvas` is built at map init, before the area polygons exist, so the services canvas sat beneath them and the semi-transparent fill washed every marker out — grey over a dark quintile, correct just off it. Services now draw into their own pane, **`srvpane` at z-index 450**, above `overlayPane` (400) and below `markerPane` (600).
- **Fix — all 99 files loaded on open.** The first load pass ran while the map was still at the national default view, where the viewport meets every bounding box. Loading is now gated by the lowest active zoom floor and capped, nearest first: 12 files in practice over Copenhagen.
- **Touch**: dots and station circles are drawn larger on a coarse pointer, which is also their hit area; the filter chips become pills with a filled on-state and the legend goes full width under 900 px.
- **Attribution** wherever the points are: each popup carries its own source line (`© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL` or `Rejseplanen, CC BY 4.0`) with the as-of date, the map footer gains a **Services:** line while the layer is on, and Market › Sources gains a *Services layer* table.
- **Licence resolved**: Rejseplanen Labs' *Retningslinjer for Labs* states **CC BY 4.0**, closing the question the probe left open. Attribution is required and sufficient; redistribution of our derived, clustered subset is allowed; no share-alike, so it does not entangle with the ODbL side.
- **Scope: points only.** No area-level service indicators — OSM density varies enough between Copenhagen and rural Jutland that a per-area rate would rank mapping effort as much as service provision. The per-kommune files are already shaped for it, so the decision is reversible without a refetch.
- Method and caveats: `docs/SERVICES.md`; counts: `docs/SERVICES_COUNTS.md`; source probe: `docs/SERVICES_PROBE.md`; integration notes: `docs/INTEGRATION_services.md`.

### Map interaction — granularity, filters and the Public layer

- **The area level now follows the zoom.** One ladder — **Country → Municipality → District → Buildings** — replaces the old two-step rule, and the map subdivides itself the way the *Zoom to <kommune>* button does on click: past zoom 10 it adopts the municipality under the middle of the screen, so Copenhagen arrives as **67 quarters** without anyone pressing a button, and a pan across a border re-adopts the new municipality. Past zoom 14 it drops to **individual buildings** where a BBR pull exists. Every rung is a level a publisher actually prints; there is **no interpolated grid**, because a number nobody published is not a figure this dashboard may draw.
- **The ladder never undoes a choice.** A municipality reached explicitly — the *Zoom to* button, a popup link, `/map/<code>` in a shared link — is not the ladder's to release, and neither is a hand-picked *Areas* / *Buildings* setting; only rungs the ladder entered are left automatically. Thresholds are asymmetric (enter at 10 and 14, leave at 9.5 and 13.5) so a half-step of zoom cannot flip the level back and forth.
- **A level label** sits at the map's bottom-left saying what is drawn — *District · Copenhagen quarters* — and what one more zoom step would give.
- **Filters for a pinned test property.** With a pin on the map a **Within: Any / 500 m / 1 km / 2 km / 5 km** control appears, and every point layer is cut to that radius: infra projects (a line or area counts as in range when any part of it is), public buildings, services and the building dots. Over Nørreport at 500 m that is services **400 → 17**, public buildings **1 338 → 125** and buildings **14 575 → 418**. It is plain great-circle arithmetic on published coordinates, it draws its own solid ring, and it lives in the hash as `rad=`, so it survives a reload, a zoom and every rung of the ladder.
- **The Public buildings layer no longer stalls on large municipalities.** Markers moved to a canvas renderer in their own pane (**`pubpane`, z-index 440**), only rows inside the padded viewport are built, and the rest are grid-clustered. DOM nodes **5 293 → 123**, a rebuild **180 ms → 73 ms**, a guarded pan **0.2 ms**. Clustering at 44 px *raised* the cost to 228 ms — 265 permanent tooltips are 265 DOM nodes — so cells are **72 px** and a cluster is labelled only from four members up: 122 clusters and 97 tooltips at zoom 13 over Copenhagen.
- **Toolbar refreshes in place.** The map header is rendered from its own function so the ladder can update the toggles, the explain block and the municipality strip without re-running `lfInit` and tearing a live map down mid-gesture.
- **Two quick-select chips removed** from the indicator bar — *Growth story* and *20–34 KBH* — and the **Population outlook card moved above the area profile** on the property card, where the strongest view in the tool belongs.

## v2.4 — 2026-09-23
- **Test property pin**: paste a Google Maps link — or just `55.67610, 12.56830` — into the box on the map toolbar and the dashboard drops a pin, drills to its municipality at postal-code level and fits the view to a 1 200 m ring. Rings at **500 / 1 000 / 1 200 m** are drawn dashed and non-interactive, so a click still reaches the polygon underneath.
- **The parser reads the link, it does not follow it** (`src/testprop.js`, 15 cases under `make test-js`). `!3d<lat>!4d<lon>` — the place pin — **wins over `@<lat>,<lon>,<zoom>z`**, which is only the viewport centre and can sit a street away; then `q=` / `ll=` / `query=`, `/search/<lat>,<lon>` and plain coordinates with a comma, semicolon or space. `+` and `%2C` are decoded first. A coarse 54–58 N / 7–16 E box catches a swapped lon/lat pair.
- **Short share links cannot be resolved in a browser** and are not pretended otherwise: `maps.app.goo.gl` and `goo.gl` return a named error asking for the long URL from the address bar. Every error is an inline line under the input — never an `alert()`.
- **The exact municipality comes from its own rings**, not from the postal code: `dist/geo/kommuner_lookup.json` (99 kommuner, simplified 0.0005°, 911 kB / 285 kB gzipped) is fetched lazily on the first pin. **Holes are kept** — Frederiksberg is a hole in København, and without it every Frederiksberg pin would be labelled København. If the fetch fails the pin falls back to the postal code's municipality and says **"approx."** rather than guessing silently. A point no polygon covers is reported as *in water or outside Denmark*.
- **Pin popup** in the existing map vocabulary: an editable label (default *Test property*, it travels in the link), kommune · postnr · Copenhagen quarter, the headline row for the pin's finest-level area, and *Analyse ›* / *Copy link* / *Remove*. The pin lives in the hash (`&pin=<lat>,<lon>&pl=<label>`), so it survives a reload, a level change and an indicator change.
- **Analysis sheet** (`#analysis?a=<lat>,<lon>&la=<label>`): one property read against every layer the dashboard already carries. The left nav group is now **Analysis**; the item opens the pin that is on the map, or an empty state with the same Google Maps box — a pin dropped there goes straight to the sheet, no detour via the map. *Analyse ›* in the pin popup lands here and the Back button returns to the map with the pin intact.
- **Sections**: an editable title with kommune · postnr · quarter and the headline row for the pin's finest-level area; a zoom-only mini map with the 500/1 000/1 200 m rings, the infrastructure overlay and the current choropleth underneath; the **area profile** — every indicator that has a value, grouped as in the dropdown, each row with a **direction-aware percentile bar** (it always fills toward "better", so a low burglary rate and a high income read the same way) next to the kommune and Denmark figures and a ↗ into Charts; **Safety** as its own card (plus `safe_pct` for Copenhagen quarters); **Infrastructure nearby**; **Public buildings** and **Schools within 1 000 m**; and **Sources & as of**.
- **Distances are computed, not looked up**: a station Point is a great-circle distance, a line or a polygon ring the nearest point on its segments, and a pin inside a development area is 0 m. Projects within 3 km are listed nearest first; a station that has **not opened** and is inside the 1 200 m ring becomes a headline chip (*M5 phase 1 · v/Prags Boulevard Øst · 200 m · 2036*). Opened projects are listed but never highlighted.

Outside the 19-kommune Copenhagen metro set both sections say so instead of showing an empty table.
- **Nothing blocks anything**: the header, map, profile and safety render from the inlined data; the per-municipality building files and the school records fill their own sections in place when they land, with a skeleton until then — no re-render, so the mini map is never torn down under the reader.

- **Fix — the Public buildings legend is never empty and never loses its categories.** Two faults, one cause: the legend was only ever written at the end of `lfPublicLayers()`, which returns early whenever the layer is off, so every re-render left the container in the DOM with no content — and `.maplegend` paints a 180 × 17 white bar. ## v2.3 — 2026-09-23
- **School quality from Uddannelsesstatistik.dk (STIL)** on the Education buildings of the Public buildings layer: FP9 grades (bundne prøver, dansk, matematik), the socioeconomic reference and its difference, elevtrivsel (generel plus the four sub-indicators), elevtal by herkomst and klassekvotient, for **364 grundskoler** in the 19 Copenhagen metro municipalities over the three latest school years (2023/24 · 2024/25 · 2025/26).
- **API discovery**: the statistics API turned out to expose a full cube catalogue at `POST /Api/v1/skema` — found via the OpenAPI spec at `/swagger/v1/swagger.json`, and documented nowhere in `/GetStarted`. `scripts/fetch_uddstat.py --skema` walks it, so no cube code in this layer was guessed.
- **The socioeconomic reference does not come from the cubes named after it.** `GS/SOCR/SOCREFEX` and `GS/SOCR/SOCREF3ÅR` are listed and return **0 rows at every detaljering tried**; `SOCR/KVALSOC` reports no dimensions at all, and the `SOCREFSIKK` emne is literally named *SocRef_Bag_Login*. The figures come from `OVER/OVERSKO` instead, which publishes the same four per afdeling. It uses a **different verdict vocabulary** — `Over niveau` / `Under niveau` / `På niveau`, not `Bedre/Dårligere end forventet` — and mapping the wrong one silently labelled all 38 schools with a significant difference as "as expected" until a browser check caught it. No 3-year reference is available, and the layer does not fake one from the 1-year figures.
- **Join**: institutionsnummer → STIL institution register coordinates → every BBR building with anvendelse **421** within 150 m (campus rule: all of them, not the nearest). 325 of 364 schools match on 421, another 24 on a 420/429 fallback at the same radius — **95.9 % with geometry, 1 236 school↔building links**. Widening the radius to 250 m was measured and rejected in favour of widening the code set. `bbr_match` records which rule fired.
- **Fixed a silent municipality drop**: the register writes `"Københavns Kommune"` (genitive), so stripping `" Kommune"` never matched `"København"` and København's 135 schools were absent from the first intake pass — its counts said 229 schools where there are 364. A trailing-"s" heuristic would break Assens, Horsens, Randers and six others, so the one exception is spelled out in `KOMMUNE_ALIAS`.
- **Indicators** in a new *Schools* group, kommune + postnr, all hidden from the chip row: `school_grade_avg`, `school_socref_diff` (new `signdec1` format — signed grade points, no % sign), `school_trivsel`, `pupils_per_school`. Area aggregates are **pupil-weighted over folkeskoler and frie grundskoler that publish, specialskoler excluded**, and each measure is weighted by *its own* denominator (grades by the pupils who sat the exams, trivsel by the pupils who answered) — which puts **17 of 19 municipalities within ±0.1 of STIL's own published kommune figure**, against 15 when weighting everything by the total roll.
- **On the map**: an Education building linked to a school gains a school block in its popup — grade with kommune and Denmark beside it, the reference difference with its significance mark, well-being, pupils, class size — and every building of a campus shows the same block and says which of N it is. New **school datasheet** (`#school/<institutionsnummer>`) with a three-year table, benchmarks and the linked buildings; a school panel per area sorted by grade; `schools 8,4 avg` on the area card's PUBLIC line; and a *Schools* trend card in Charts per selected municipality against Denmark.
- **Grade colour mode**: with the public filter on Education alone (`&public=1&pub=edu`) the Education markers take a 5-step blue ramp by FP9 grade, quintiles over the loaded municipalities, and the legend switches to *FP9 grade avg*. **A school with no grade keeps the base Education hue, hollow with a thin outline, never the bottom bin** — 0.–6. klasse schools, specialskoler and suppressed cells are not low-scoring schools.
- **Suppression is not zero**: a cell under 3 observations (under 5 pupils for trivsel and the reference) is *omitted* from the API response, so a missing school can only be counted from the outside. Coverage is 74 % of schools for grades, 70 % for the reference, 54 % for trivsel; every dash in the UI carries a tooltip saying it is suppressed, never a 0.
- Licence: free reuse including commercial, attribution **"Kilde: Uddannelsesstatistik.dk"** plus retrieval date — carried on every popup, sheet, panel and chart, and in the sources table.
- **Verification** (`docs/DATA_MAP.md` §7): five schools — Skolen på Duevej, Lindevangskolen, Den Classenske Legatskole, Krebs' Skole and Nørre Fælled Skole — re-queried one at a time straight from the API on 2026-09-23. All 20 comparisons identical to `data/processed/schools.json`, including a suppressed well-being cell that is absent in both and both significance directions.
